# Multiplayer prisoner exchange

The approved G4 policy implements exchange as an online domain operation around the unchanged canonical engine. Local play and AI captors cannot negotiate. Only the two connected human captors whose armies are alive may propose, accept or reject. The frozen prisoners themselves cannot negotiate.

## Historical source and adopted extension

[Chris Zalewski, Enochian Chess of the Golden Dawn (1994), rule 8.5, printed page 96 / PDF page 126](https://www.labirintoermetico.com/06Numerologia_Cabala/Zalewski-Enochian-Chess-of-the-Golden-Dawn.pdf#page=126) describes the second captor proposing to the opponent who captured the captor's allied king. The opponent may refuse; proposals can be repeated. The source permits that second captor to offer immediately after capture or on their subsequent turns, returns rescued kings to their own thrones (or nearby empty unthreatened squares when occupied), thaws their remaining pieces, and excludes frozen negotiators.

[Approved G4](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-enochian-multiplayer-design.md) additionally permits either eligible captor to propose on their own later turns. This broadening of subsequent-turn proposals is an adopted multiplayer extension. Immediate proposals remain limited to the second qualifying capture. Human connectivity requirements, owner and revision bindings, the 60-second lifetime, safety on an otherwise empty throne, simultaneous safety with both armies thawed, king-step distance and deterministic tie resolution are also adopted online policy. They are not claims about unambiguous historical wording.

The executable geometry has external thrones: Y `(0,-1)`, B `(-1,7)`, R `(7,8)`, K `(8,0)`. It permits one piece per square. Exchange does not import historical throne sharing, check constraints on ordinary moves, concourses, new promotion behavior, or additional victory rules.

## Placement and finality

Both restored kings are considered together. For each prisoner, try its empty safe own throne first; otherwise consider the 64 ordinary squares ordered by Chebyshev distance from that throne (king-step distance), then row and column. Prisoners are processed in fixed engine color order `R,B,Y,K`, regardless of offer direction. Compare candidate pairs lexicographically in that order. Other armies' external thrones are not fallback squares.

Each safety probe has both armies thawed and both kings placed, using the canonical `isSquareAttacked` function and canonical teams. This detects restored enemy kings threatening each other and attacks by previously frozen pieces, including pawn captures against the newly occupied squares. Frozen pieces remain blockers according to canonical movement. Squares are never shared. A placed king can block a sliding attack; safety is assessed on the complete proposed board.

The search has at most 65 candidates per prisoner, including their external throne: at most **4225 pairs** (4096 ordinary-board pairs plus throne combinations). Keeping the external geometry means a complete search cannot promise only 4096 total pairs. There is no truncated search or unbounded retry. If no safe pair exists, `placement_failed` with `reason: no_safe_pair` leaves the candidate entirely unchanged, including the pending offer and prisoner ledger.

Acceptance restores only the two kings, thaws their armies, removes their prisoner entries, and clears the offer. Previously removed non-king pieces remain removed. Turn index and move count stay unchanged because exchange is a separate negotiated operation. A canonical terminal move wins its race with exchange if committed first: phase, terminal-result and engine-over guards reject acceptance, including acceptance within a casual return allowance. Terminal outcomes remain final.

## Domain integration contract

`server/src/domain/exchange.ts` exports private `ExchangeRecord`, `Prisoner`, `ExchangeOffer`, server-authenticated `ExchangeActor`, structured `ExchangeResult`, `EXCHANGE_OFFER_MS`, and immutable `THRONES`. Helpers mutate **candidate clones only**. They perform no persistence, authentication, clock reads, scheduling or revision increments. Callers must use authoritative server context, absolute server time and revision compare-and-swap, and publish only after durable acceptance.

- After a canonical move, assign the engine successor, advance the candidate revision once, call `invalidateExchange(candidate, 'move')`, and call `recordKingCaptures(candidate, successor.events)` with the new capture revision. Only canonical king-capture events create prisoner entries; capture owner identities are retained privately. The optional third argument supplies an explicit capture revision if needed. The event source must be trusted, not client-supplied. Replay with the same capture revision is idempotent.
- `offerExchange(candidate, actor, { offerId, counterpart })` verifies the captor relationship, current owners and human eligibility. `actor.expectedRevision` must equal the loaded record revision. A successful offer binds both private prisoner records and sets `guardRevision` to **loaded revision + 1**, the revision in which that offer will be committed. The caller then increments the candidate revision exactly once and persists it with CAS. A failed helper must not be persisted as an accepted command. Reusing an active offer is rejected as `offer_pending`.
- `acceptExchange(candidate, actor, offerId)` and `rejectExchange(candidate, actor, offerId)` require the authorized counterpart, the current record revision matching both actor and offer guards, an unexpired offer, unchanged private owner/prisoner bindings and current eligibility of both captors. On success, increment the candidate revision exactly once and commit atomically. Duplicate command replay remains the enclosing command processor/store's responsibility; a second fresh acceptance sees `no_offer`.
- `expireExchange(candidate, now)` clears at `now >= expiresAt`, even if the scheduler runs late. Acceptance itself checks the absolute deadline and never relies on the scheduler. Persist expiry changes through CAS. `invalidateExchange(candidate, reason)` clears after **any** move, control/ownership/connectivity change, pause or end. A revision change also makes an uncleared offer unusable. The reason union is `move | control_change | pause | end`.
- `publicExchange(record)` returns an explicit public allowlist: offer ID, proposer/counterpart colors, prisoner colors, guard revision and expiry. Private owner IDs and capture IDs never enter synchronized state. It creates a fresh projection. The parent snapshot serializer must explicitly add this projection; never spread `ExchangeRecord` or `ExchangeOffer`.

`multiplayer: false` always returns `local_mode`. Both permanent and temporary bots are ineligible. Dead or frozen captors return `frozen_captor`; absent prisoners or changed bindings cannot restore a king. Actor identities, controller and multiplayer context must come from the server, never from the payload. The enclosing processor must durably deduplicate request IDs, serialize commands and reject revision races through CAS before publishing either an offer or an exchange.

## Server command and public transport

The room accepts `exchange_command` with exactly `{ requestId, matchId, expectedRevision, protocolVersion: 1, rulesVersion: 'enochian-current-1', action }`. Allowed actions are `{ type: 'offer', counterpart: Color }`, `{ type: 'accept', offerId }`, and `{ type: 'reject', offerId }`. An offer's ID is its request ID. Unknown fields, oversized envelopes, invalid identifiers and incompatible versions are rejected. Room requests must target that room's match, and authentication supplies the actor; owner IDs and placements are never client action fields.

`ExchangeService` uses actor-scoped durable request replay before checking current revision or changing expiry state. Fresh requests check expiry before validation; an offer expires at the absolute boundary even when the timer runs late. Expiry itself is a CAS-persisted lifecycle event. A command racing that expiry can receive `stale_revision` and must refresh. Accepted offer/reject/accept transitions each persist one successor revision and their event before acknowledgment or publication. Placement failure returns `placement_failed` with `reason: no_safe_pair` and persists no partial transition. The compact additional acknowledgment codes are `placement_failed`, `no_offer`, and `offer_pending`; other failures use the existing authorization, phase, deadline, frozen-army and revision codes.

Canonical moves invalidate offers and record king capture metadata in the same committed successor. Casual departure, bot expiry and reclaim clear pending offers; service recovery, pause, controller changes and terminal transitions clear them. Room maintenance and incoming message preflight persist delayed expiry. No online exchange endpoint is installed in the local/scaffold transport path.

Public snapshots include `exchangeOffer` and `exchangeAvailable`, an explicit color-only proposer-to-counterpart map such as `{ R: 'B' }`. Availability uses the same read-only eligibility function as proposal validation, including capture/owner bindings, human connectivity, alive armies and timing. A current pending offer suppresses new proposal hints. This is advisory UI data; the server always validates commands again.

Schema synchronizes pending offers through the scalar fields `exchangeId`, `exchangeProposer`, `exchangeCounterpart`, `exchangeGuardRevision`, `exchangeExpiresAt`, `exchangePrisonerFirst`, and `exchangePrisonerSecond`. Empty strings and `-1` represent no offer. Each public seat additionally exposes `exchangeCounterpart`, a color or empty string, from `exchangeAvailable`. No prisoner ledger, owner ID, hash or capture ID is synchronized.

Integrated tests use the memory CAS store and a real SDK connection to verify durable capture/offer/acceptance, actor-scoped replay, competing acceptance commits, strict expiry, terminal finality, recovery invalidation, disconnect clearance and Schema privacy. Browser controls are added separately in Task 12.

Tests cover the capture ledger, identity projection, immediate/subsequent proposal timing, wrong captors, local mode, frozen/dead armies, AI captors, changed owners/controllers, strict deadline expiry, rejection/reproposal, invalidation, terminal races, duplicate fresh acceptance, external thrones, occupied and threatened throne fallback, ties, restored-army threats, conflicting king squares and failure without partial mutation.
